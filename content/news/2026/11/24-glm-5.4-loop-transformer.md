There is a simple version of transformer scaling: more layers, more parameters, more capable model. GLM-5.4, [released yesterday by Z.ai](https://the-decoder.com/z-ai-follows-openai-into-loop-transformer-territory-with-its-new-750b-flagship-model), argues for a different axis. It is a 750-billion-parameter model that runs a block of 28 transformer layers not once, but twice — refining the intermediate representation on the second pass before producing output.

Z.ai calls this a loop transformer, and they are not alone in the architecture. Reports suggest OpenAI has moved in the same direction. The basic idea is that depth and parameters are not the same resource. A 54-layer model that loops 28 of those layers has the same parameter budget as a 54-layer standard transformer, but the effective compute path the data travels is longer — 82 layers' worth of operations, not 54. With per-token gating controlling how much each pass modifies the hidden state, and a KV cache that is shared across the two iterations, the model can decide how much to refine rather than applying full depth uniformly.

Whether this is better than simply training a 100-layer transformer is an open question. The obvious concern is that sharing weights across iterations forces those layers to serve two different roles in the forward pass, which may prevent the kind of specialized, layer-specific representations standard transformers develop. Z.ai's answer, published in the architecture paper for a 30B test version, is that it isn't worse: they report better numbers than their baseline on most benchmarks, with the clearest gain on AIME25 math (from 19.2 to 30.4). The 750B model's benchmarks are absent entirely — Z.ai says evaluations of the full model are "ongoing."

That gap between architecture claim and actual numbers for the flagship is worth flagging. The 30B ablation is evidence the design is not broken, but the pitch — that GLM-5.4 matches models with two to three trillion parameters — rests on nothing published yet. Z.ai is a credible lab; GLM-5 has been competitive. But it is a claim, not a measurement.

Availability is similarly deferred. GLM-5.4 is live through Z.ai's own coding product and chat interface, with output at 70–80 tokens per second under load and 200 off-peak. The API is "coming soon," pricing is not announced, and weights are promised after safety testing. At 750B parameters, running this locally would require infrastructure most people don't have — the open weights matter mostly for independent evaluation and fine-tuning, not casual local inference.

What is not deferred is the architectural signal. The loop transformer as a design pattern is now represented by at least two major labs, and the motivation is fairly straightforward: once you have trained a large model and are looking for more capability, adding more parameters is expensive and often hits diminishing returns. Adding compute depth through iteration costs inference time, not training budget growth. It is a different efficiency curve than mixture-of-experts, which reduces active parameters per token. These approaches are not mutually exclusive — GLM-5.4 combines looping with sparse attention — and the industry seems to be exploring combinations rather than betting on any single trick.

The concrete claims will be checkable once the API ships and the broader evaluation community gets access to the 750B model. Until then, the architecture itself is the story: a large-scale production deployment of a looped depth design, by a lab that has been building this family for several years.
